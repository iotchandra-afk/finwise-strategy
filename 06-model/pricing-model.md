# The Model | FinWise

> **Module 6: Pricing & Monetization**  
> **Recommendation:** Keep the reverse trial, but make the commercial boundary correspond to *verified recurring decision value*, not access to a reassuring-looking dashboard. Test **$59 per business/month** for an **Owner Plus** subscription; it is a **candidate price, not a validated price or existing FinWise offer**.  
> **Decision status:** Conditional packaging and pricing recommendation. No current FinWise price list, account-level behavior, willingness-to-pay evidence, connector costs or gross-margin data were supplied.

## 1. Stage + model: product-market fit is not revenue-model fit

**Stage:** The case says FinWise has reached product-market fit and is attempting the next phase of **product-led revenue expansion**. Its baseline is **2% trial-to-paid conversion** and **40% one-year paid-logo retention**, with paid advertising funding most trial acquisition. These numbers show weak monetization and survival; they do **not** establish whether the obstacle is first value, customer fit, a reverse-trial handoff, recurring need or price.

**Model:** An explicit **reverse trial → transparent Starter state or Owner Plus subscription**, charged **per business workspace per month**, not per user, login, alert, invoice or financial transaction. The owner buys continuity around *one operating decision*: What changed in the cash position, what is defensible, and what must I reconsider before the obligation cutoff?

**Ideal first buyer, to validate:** An owner-operated small business with variable customer receipts, near-term payroll/vendor commitments and no dedicated finance team. Do not impose the same offer on accountants managing many entities or companies requiring treasury controls; their users, authorization models and economics differ.

### The economic logic

The case's aggregate rates imply, **conditionally for compatible cohorts**, `1,000 trials × 2% paid conversion × 40% one-year retention = eight retained payers`. That is *illustrative durable-logo yield*, not an observed matched cohort or revenue forecast. At a **hypothetical $59 per paying business/month**, 20 converted accounts would represent $1,180 in monthly subscription run-rate **at a point when all 20 are active**; eight retained accounts would represent $472 at the one-year landmark **if all eight are still at $59**. Neither figure estimates first-year revenue, LTV, margin or CAC payback. The large uncertainty is duration and cost, not arithmetic.

**Strategic choice:** Make the next pricing investment contingent on demonstrating *recurring customer value*, not on discounting 98% of trials into the plan or extracting more from an unverified job.

## 2. Recommendation: charge for continuity, not for a safety warning

| Offer | Customer promise and entitlements — **proposed, not current features** | Pricing disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reverse trial — full experience, fixed disclosed duration** | If source health permits, complete the first verified cash decision; save its evidence and assumptions; experience a changed-facts review when a valid opportunity occurs. Show the exact end date, future entitlement changes and paid price **before** sensitive data are connected. | No-charge trial; actual length **must be audited**, not invented. Do not silently begin charging. |
| **Starter — explicit fallback** | View, export and correct owned decision history; run a limited, **clearly labeled point-in-time** scenario with permitted/manual evidence. Display its source timestamp, any critical unverified obligations and **“not continuously monitored”** when live tracking is absent. | No charge; enforce cost-conscious limits only after validating connector and support economics. Never imply passive coverage it cannot deliver. |
| **Owner Plus — primary paid offer** | Ongoing supported and permissioned source updates, Decision Delta comparisons against saved decisions, exception/reconciliation visibility, review history and controlled notifications when qualified material events affect decisions. Core promise: less reconstruction of recurring cash choices. | **$59/business/month candidate anchor**, monthly cancelability. Separately test **$39 and $89** offers under a controlled price study. No per-alert or per-seat meter. |
| **Advisor / multi-business workspace — gated option, not launch tier** | Explicit client delegation, per-entity data isolation, audit and multi-client workflow, only if a distinct accountant job and acquisition economics are proven. | **Unpriced / uncommitted** until demand, permission model and unit costs are known; never infer this segment from owner behavior. |

**Paywall placement:** Put the premium boundary around the **ongoing monitored review service** *after the trial's first defensible decision*. Do not gate the customer's own historical records, source caveats, the last known critical warning, export or correction behind a payment prompt. The paid product is *verified continuity*; the free product is a truthful, bounded snapshot. It must be visually unmistakable when monitoring has stopped. If the customer never reached a usable first decision because of data limitations, do not market that as consumed trial value; investigate a safe grace/assisted recovery policy and its cost rather than resetting trials indiscriminately.

**Boundary conditions:** A “paid” invoice is not bank-settled and available cash; an import is not a reconciled cash position; and an automated alert is not authorization to delay payroll, borrow, or move funds. The product must continue to block false **all-clear** conclusions on stale funds, unverified ACH cutoffs, missing payroll taxes or critical obligations. Essential safety context, privacy controls, and deletion/export must not be premium upsells. If trustworthy continuous monitoring is not technically supportable, **do not sell Owner Plus as continuous monitoring** until that contract is met.

## 3. Why per-business subscription — and what would reverse it

1. **The value is an operating decision, not a button pressed.** A per-alert price would penalize engagement at consequential moments and incentivize unsafe alert volume. A per-seat price penalizes inviting the bookkeeper into an auditable decision. A percentage of cash or invoice volume confuses liquidity with value and creates unpredictable bills.
2. **A subscription is justified only if the decision recurs.** Module 3's Decision Delta should earn a verified second review; a quiet week may correctly produce zero alerts. Do not optimize notification volume or daily activity to manufacture subscription engagement.
3. **Unit economics determine whether the promise is scalable.** Live banking/data access, reconciliation, exception handling, notification operations and support may be material variable costs. Paid status alone is not a profitable customer.

**Decision reversal:** If well-matched customers make one important decision but have few subsequent material events, an always-on plan may be the wrong value metric or even the wrong initial segment. Study a time-bounded cash-planning package or annual/seasonal service *with explicit monitoring limits*; do **not** pretend an episodic job is recurring by sending reminders. If recurring use is strong but customers reject the fee at the paywall, test entitlement/price fit. If neither first value nor data readiness is reliable, pause monetization optimization and repair the product.

## 4. Validation sequence: separate product efficacy from pricing elasticity

| Gate | Evidence to gather | Decision it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Audit actual offer and economics** | Current trial duration and entitlements, conversion by time of paywall, existing ARPA/discounts, churn reasons, bank/connector costs, support minutes, payment failures, acquisition source, real cohort maturity. | Establish a true pricing baseline and exclude accidental downgrade surprises or unprofitable plans. |
| **B. Demonstrate the value being sold** | Account-level verified decision (L1), second verified review (L2), event opportunity, data readiness, qualitative willingness to depend on the answer. Module 5's onboarding A/B happens **only** if pre-value friction is verified. | Establish that paying for recurring decision continuity is a plausible job, rather than charging for setup or a single simulation. |
| **C. Diagnose willingness to pay** | Interview owners who achieved value but declined to pay, buyers who retained, and recent churners. Blind concept/package tests and a price-sensitivity exercise using **$39 / $59 / $89 hypothetical anchors**; probe actual alternatives, not just stated enthusiasm. | Refine what belongs in Plus and the defensible price range. Interview responses are **not** measured conversion elasticity. |
| **D. Test the commercial offer separately** | After product value and safety are credible, run a preregistered business-account randomized **offer/price** test with honest, consistent displayed terms and controlled channels. Do **not** change onboarding and price in the same experiment. Measure paid conversion, net revenue per eligible trial, refunds, 90-day paid survival and support/connector contribution. | Select or reject a price/package; do not declare a winner solely on higher immediate conversion or a short-lived discount. |
| **E. Prove retention and contribution** | Mature trial-start and paid-start cohorts with 90/365-day paid survival, paid review opportunity and contribution after data and support costs. Compare channels and matched cohorts, not only enthusiastic activated users. | Decide whether to scale organic/permissioned accountant acquisition or keep paid acquisition constrained. |

**Precommitted commercial scorecard:**

- **Primary commercial outcome:** **90-day net contribution per eligible trial start**, measured from a common start cohort once prices, connector fees, billing costs and service load are known; paid revenue per trial and paid conversion are explanatory components. For longer horizons, report retained paid businesses per 1,000 qualified trials and 365-day paid survival. Do not use first-month MRR alone.
- **Leading product outcomes:** VDA7, verified second decision review by day 28, and qualified review opportunity; analyze reverse-trial entitlement expiry as a distinct event.
- **Safety / trust stop rules:** Any consequential false spendable-cash assurance or material confidentiality breach stops the affected release; monitor missed obligation cutoffs, stale-data decisions, consent failures, refunds, complaint rate and support burden. No revenue uplift offsets an unsafe financial verdict.
- **Unit economic hurdle — explicitly a management hypothesis:** At the provisional $59 plan, a **70% account-level contribution margin target** would require recurring variable cost (data/connectors, payment processing, reconciliation and support) **at or below $17.70 per business/month**. That is a proposed hurdle, **not known FinWise economics**; revise it with real acquisition, fixed costs, retention and support distributions. Use paid CAC payback only when observed acquisition cost and survival justify it.

### Cohort arithmetic that matters more than list price

`Contribution per eligible trial start = paid-conversion probability × expected discounted paid contribution over observed tenure − trial servicing cost − attributable acquisition cost`

The second term can be material if free trials require premium live connectors and manual reconciliation. A price increase that slightly lifts ARPA but reduces 90-day survival or demands excessive service can make growth worse. Likewise, applying the 40% annual retention rate as a uniform monthly churn hazard or estimating LTV from it alone would be unjustified.

## 5. Operating decisions and 12-month sequence

| Window | Product / commercial decision | Continue only if… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–60 days: establish truth** | Audit funnels, terms and unit costs; interview three behavioral groups; instrument actual decision quality; fix material evidence defects. | Customer and cost data can distinguish no first value, no repeat job, offer mismatch and wrong acquisition. |
| **60–120 days: establish repeat value** | If H1 is supported, run Module 5's gated onboarding experiment; separately validate Decision Delta safety and genuine second reviews. | Improvement is in **verified decisions**, not mere clicks, with safety intact. |
| **120–240 days: earn a price** | Test value metric, Owner Plus entitlement contract and candidate prices independently of onboarding; observe first commercial cohort maturity. | Paying owners can articulate repeated value and account-level contribution is viable. |
| **240–365 days: scale or redirect** | Expand the most effective segment/distribution only after cohort paid survival and contribution improve. Explore accountant referrals with consent; no preemptive “viral” claim. | Higher **retained profitable payers per qualified trial** persist; channel economics improve without worsening trust. |

**Call I would make as Senior PM:** Approve the **per-business, monitored-decision subscription architecture for validation**, **not** the $59 price for unrestricted launch. Fund the instrumentation, data-integrity and buyer research first. Release a commercial offer only with a truthful entitlement contract and a passed contribution gate. If verified repeated value fails to emerge, change the target job or revenue model instead of applying a price promotion to a retention problem.
